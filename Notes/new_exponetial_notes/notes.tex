Our problem is to solve
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation*}
    F''(z)=2ik^2\varphi^2\,e^{kz/2}F(z).
\end{empheq}

Define
$$
\lambda^2=2i\,k^2\varphi^2, \quad\text{ i.e. } \lambda=\left(1+i\right)k\varphi,
$$

so that
$$
F''(z)=\lambda^2 e^{kz/2}F(z).
$$

Introduce the new variable
$$
t=\frac{4\lambda}{k}e^{kz/4}.
$$

Then
$$
\frac{dt}{dz}=\frac{k}{4}t,
$$

and therefore
$$
\frac{dF}{dz}=\frac{k}{4}t\frac{dF}{dt},
$$

$$
\frac{d^2F}{dz^2}
=\left(\frac{k}{4}\right)^2
\left(
t^2\frac{d^2F}{dt^2}
+t\frac{dF}{dt}
\right).
$$

Since
$$
e^{kz/2}
=
\left(\frac{xk}{4\lambda}\right)^2,
$$

substitution into the differential equation yields
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation*}
    t^2\frac{d^2F}{dt^2}
+t\frac{dF}{dt}
-t^2F=0.
\end{empheq}

This is the modified Bessel equation of order zero,
$$
t^2y''+xy'-(t^2+\nu^2)y=0,
\qquad \nu=0.
$$

Hence the general solution is
$$
F(z)=A\,I_0(t)+B\,K_0(t).
$$

The boundary condition
$$
F(\infty)=0
$$

implies ($A=0$), since ($I_0(t)$) grows exponentially as
($t\to\infty$), whereas ($K_0(t)$) decays exponentially. Thus
$$
F(z)=B\,K_0(t).
$$

Differentiating gives
$$
F'(z)
=
-\frac{k}{4}BxK_1(t),
$$

where we used
$$
\frac{dK_0}{dt}=-K_1.
$$

Applying the second boundary condition
$$
F'(0)=-ifU_g
$$

and using
$$
x_0=\left.t\right|_{z=0}=\frac{4\lambda}{k},
$$

one finds
$$
-\frac{k}{4}B\,x_0K_1(x_0)
=
-ifU_g,
$$

so that
$$
B
=
\frac{ifU_g}
{\lambda\,K_1(x_0)}.
$$

Therefore,
$$
F(z)
=
\frac{ifU_g}
{\lambda \,K_1(x_0)}
K_0(t),
$$

with
$$
t=x_0e^{kz/4},
\qquad
x_0=\frac{4\lambda}{k}=4\left(1+i\right)\varphi 
\qquad \implies 
t=4\left(1+i\right)\varphi e^{kz/4}.
$$

Substituting ($\lambda=\left(1+i\right)k\varphi$) and using $i/(1+i)=(1+i)/2$finally yields
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation*}
    F(z)
    =
    \frac{\left(1+i\right)fU_g}
    {2k\varphi}
    \frac{K_0\left(4\left[1+i\right]\varphi e^{kz/4}\right)}{K_1\left(4\left[1+i\right]\varphi\right)}
\end{empheq}
