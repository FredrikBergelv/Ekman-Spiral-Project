Our problem is to solve
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation*}
    F''(z)=2ik^2\varphi^2\,e^{kz}F(z).
\end{empheq}

Define
$$
\lambda^2=2i\,k^2\varphi^2, \quad\text{i.e.}\quad \lambda=(1+i)k\varphi,
$$

so that
$$
F''(z)=\lambda^2 e^{kz}F(z).
$$

Introduce the new variable
$$
t=\frac{2\lambda}{k}e^{kz/2}.
$$

Then
$$
\frac{dt}{dz}=\frac{k}{2}t,
$$

and therefore
$$
\frac{dF}{dz}=\frac{k}{2}t\frac{dF}{dt},
$$

$$
\frac{d^2F}{dz^2}
=
\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)^2
\left(
t^2\frac{d^2F}{dt^2}
+t\frac{dF}{dt}
\right).
$$

Since
$$
e^{kz}
=
\left(\frac{tk}{2\lambda}\right)^2,
$$

substitution into the differential equation yields
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation*}
    t^2\frac{d^2F}{dt^2}
+t\frac{dF}{dt}
-t^2F=0.
\end{empheq}

This is the modified Bessel equation of order zero,
$$
t^2y''+ty'-(t^2+\nu^2)y=0,
\qquad \nu=0.
$$

Hence the general solution is
$$
F(z)=A\,I_0(t)+B\,K_0(t).
$$

The boundary condition
$$
F(\infty)=0
$$

implies ($A=0$), since $I_0(t)$ grows exponentially as
$t\to\infty$, whereas $K_0(t)$ decays exponentially. Thus
$$
F(z)=B\,K_0(t).
$$

Differentiating gives
$$
F'(z)
=
-\frac{k}{2}BtK_1(t),
$$

where we used
$$
\frac{dK_0}{dt}=-K_1.
$$

Applying the second boundary condition
$$
F'(0)=-ifU_g
$$

and using
$$
t_0=\left.t\right|_{z=0}=\frac{2\lambda}{k},
$$

one finds
$$
-\frac{k}{2}B\,t_0K_1(t_0)
=
-ifU_g,
$$

so that
$$
B
=
\frac{ifU_g}
{\lambda\,K_1(t_0)}.
$$

Therefore,
$$
F(z)
=
\frac{ifU_g}
{\lambda \,K_1(t_0)}
K_0(t),
$$

with
$$
t=t_0e^{kz/2},
\qquad
t_0=\frac{2\lambda}{k}=2(1+i)\varphi
\qquad \implies
t=2(1+i)\varphi e^{kz/2}.
$$

Substituting $\lambda=(1+i)k\varphi$ and using
$$
\frac{i}{1+i}=\frac{1+i}{2},
$$
finally yields
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation*}
F(z)
=
\frac{(1+i)fU_g}
{2k\varphi}
\frac{K_0\!\left(2(1+i)\varphi e^{kz/2}\right)}
{K_1\!\left(2(1+i)\varphi\right)}.
\end{empheq}
